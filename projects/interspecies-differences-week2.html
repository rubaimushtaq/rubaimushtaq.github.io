Interindividual Behavioral Variation Post-Predation: Week 2 ​

Introduction ​

In social invertebrate colonies, individual behavioral differences may either remain fixed across fluctuating ecological contexts (representing static personality syndromes) or adjust dynamically following energetic inputs, predatory stress, or foraging success.

In this follow-up investigation, surviving individuals from the Week 1 Stegodyphus cohort were maintained communally for seven days, during which they constructed a dense communal silk retreat web. We introduced live prey (Tribolium castaneum and Tenebrio molitor larvae) to stimulate vibration-guided communal predatory responses, followed by post-foraging re-assessment of individual boldness and aggression scores.

Hypothesis: Relative rank-order personality scores remain stable across repeated trials, with no fundamental realignment of behavioral syndromes post-predation.

Materials and Methods ​

Materials ​

  • 4 surviving velvet spiders from Week 1 cohort (2 individuals died prior to Week 2 testing)
  • Live insect prey: Tribolium castaneum larva (small), Tenebrio molitor larva (large)
  • Stereomicroscope, fine forceps, blunt tactile probes
  • Digital stopwatch

Methods ​

Spiders were monitored in their communal webbed arena. A T. castaneum larva was positioned at the dense central retreat hub, and behavioral interactions were recorded for 25 minutes. A larger T. molitor larva was subsequently introduced, with observations recorded for an additional 25 minutes.

Following prey capture interactions, spiders were separated into individual chambers and re-tested using the identical pneumatic boldness assay and tactile aggression scale established in Week 1.

Discussion ​

Behavioral Specialization During Prey Encounters ​

During live prey exposure, individuals displayed consistent division of labor:

  • Bob (Spider 6, Yellow): Showed the highest aggression score (6/6, leg raise) and served as the sole primary attacker, actively stalking, immobilizing, and envenomating the prey.
  • Shrek II (Spider 4, Green): Exhibited persistent locomotor activity, engaging in web stabilization and wrapping the prey items in silk sheets.
  • Olaf (Spider 1) and Nemo (Spider 3): Remained quiescent in retreat chambers, exhibiting lowest aggression scores.

Temporal Personality Stability ​

Every surviving individual exhibited a minor, uniform reduction in boldness score post-trial (Δ≈−1 to −33 s), reflecting metabolic fatigue from web reconstruction and prey handling. Despite subtle shifts in absolute magnitude, the relative rank-order hierarchy was strictly preserved across both timepoints, validating the hypothesis that personality syndromes in social spiders represent durable individual traits.
