Patterns of Morphological Variation: Geometric Morphometrics ​

Introduction ​

Organisms from the same species often exhibit marked morphological variation arising from differences in genetic background, developmental plasticity, and environmental constraints. To quantify anatomical shifts, biological researchers employ morphometrics: either traditional linear morphometrics (comparing distance metrics such as body length or width) or multidimensional landmark-based geometric morphometrics, which separates pure shape deformation from uniform isotropic scaling.

In this investigation, we evaluated morphological differentiation in Drosophila melanogaster reared under chronic nutritional stress (malnutrition) versus control diets, assessing whether landmark-based geometric morphometrics provides superior discriminative resolution compared to traditional linear metrics.

Materials and Methods ​

Materials ​

  • Female D. melanogaster from control (C) and selected malnourished (S) lineages
  • Left wings mounted on glass microscope slides
  • Stereomicroscope coupled to high-resolution digital imaging apparatus
  • ImageJ (linear trait calibration and coordinate extraction)
  • MorphoJ (Generalized Procrustes Analysis and Principal Component Analysis)

Methods ​

Traditional Linear Morphometrics ​

Head width and thorax length were measured on calibrated stereomicrographs (N=6 per treatment: C01–C06 and S01–S06) to capture macro-level somatic size variation.

Control female specimen (0.5 mm scale bar)Selected malnourished female specimen (0.5 mm scale bar)

Geometric Morphometrics Protocol ​

Left wings from 24 female specimens (12 control: C01L–C12L; 12 malnourished: S01L–S12L) were dissected, mounted flat, and imaged under uniform magnification.

Mounted left wing from control lineMounted left wing from malnourished line

Eleven homologous anatomical landmarks were digitized at standardized vein junctions and wing margins across all specimens:


Figure: Configuration of the 11 digitized anatomical landmarks on the Drosophila wing blade.

Procrustes Alignment & Principal Component Analysis (PCA) ​

Raw coordinates were translated, scaled to unit centroid size, and iteratively rotated via Generalized Procrustes Analysis (GPA) to isolate pure shape variation. PCA was executed on the Procrustes covariance matrix in MorphoJ to identify orthogonal axes of coordinated morphological variance.

Head width exhibited severe, non-overlapping reduction: the lower quartile of control flies (0.333 mm) exceeded the upper quartile of malnourished flies (0.316 mm), demonstrating marked developmental canalization failure under nutritional starvation.

Geometric Morphometric Eigenanalysis ​

PCA resolved 18 principal components spanning the shape tangent space:

  • PC1: Accounts for 47.00% of total shape variance.
  • PC2: Accounts for 22.75% of total shape variance.
  • Combined, PC1 and PC2 explain 69.76% (~70%) of all shape divergence.

Shape Space Projections & Outlier Analysis ​

PC1 vs PC2 scores: Control (red) vs Malnourished (cyan)Individual specimen labels highlighting geometric outliers

Four specimens (C010L, S03L, S09L, C06L) occupy extreme coordinates outside the main cluster due to localized mounting distortion or minor margin tearing at Landmark 1.

Evaluating the core cluster of 20 specimens reveals consistent subtle biological divergence:

  • Control specimens: Predominantly positive PC1 scores (mean PC1≈+0.0078).
  • Malnourished specimens: Predominantly negative PC1 scores (mean PC1≈−0.0078).


Figure: Landmark displacement vectors associated with Principal Component 1.

Eigenvector loadings indicate that PC1 is primarily driven by horizontal displacement of Landmark 1 (x1 loading = -0.7648), reflecting subtle allometric narrowing and proximal elongation of the wing margin under nutritional deficit.

Conclusion ​

Both geometric morphometrics and traditional linear measurements detected significant morphological divergence under starvation selection. While geometric morphometrics resolved subtle allometric wing shape deformations, traditional head width measurements exhibited stark, non-overlapping diagnostic separation, demonstrating that simple linear metrics remain exceptionally powerful and sensitive indicators of systemic developmental stress.
